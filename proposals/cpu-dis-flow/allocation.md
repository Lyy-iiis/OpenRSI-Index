# Does the choice of refinement location matter?

The candidate and thresholds are frozen at c4ed93a. This follow-up adds a paired control to identify the effect of location selection, then tests transfer to a second dataset. The 40-pair Sintel development split is used to exercise the protocol. The original Sintel holdout has already been scored. The Middlebury comparison uses every public pair with a flow label, one pair for each of eight scenes. RubberWhale was previously used to time stock OpenCV before the candidate was developed. Report the seven other scenes separately as a sensitivity check.

For every image pair, measure how many valid grid centers the residual gate selects. Give three controls the same count and sample exactly that many eligible centers without replacement using seeds 17, 29 and 43. The controls receive the count and their selection masks before timing. The controls see neither the original mask's locations nor any label. All other inference steps are identical. Check the counts at runtime. The comparison matches expensive center checks exactly. The number of distinct valid neighbors and the wall time can still vary. Record measured latency for each arm. Mask preparation is free for the random controls, giving them a favorable timing comparison.

Compare pooled EPE of FAST, the residual gate and the arithmetic mean of the three random-control EPEs. Keep individual seed results and count scene-level wins as well. Report the share of the improvement over FAST attributable to location selection as (random EPE − residual EPE) / (FAST EPE − residual EPE) only when the denominator is positive. Use signed EPE differences when the candidate misses the baseline. The Sintel boundary metric remains available. Middlebury supplies a valid-flow domain but no matching occlusion masks in these two archives; it contributes only whole-image EPE. Use a fresh process for each measured split, nine repeats per pair and the same single-thread timing convention as the pilot. Repeat Middlebury in another process to report timing variability. With eight pairs its nearest-rank P95 is the slowest pair median.

A transferable gain requires the residual gate to beat both FAST and the mean random control in pooled Middlebury EPE, to beat FAST in a strict majority of the eight scenes, and to stay within the pilot's 10% P95 allowance on both Middlebury runs. Report failed criteria individually. No candidate or gate tuning follows observation of Middlebury results.
